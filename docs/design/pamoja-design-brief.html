Design brief · for Claude Design

Pamoja

The fan companion app for the PAMOJA Pass at AFCON 2027 — one digital pass used across Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania for match access, travel and city services. This brief asks for a mobile and desktop redesign with usability and navigation as the priority.

What already works and must survive: the two-hue palette, Outfit + JetBrains Mono, the Mount Kenya media motif, and the product's rule that Pamoja never holds a fan's money. Everything below the "Problems" heading is open.

What the app is

A React Native / Expo app (iOS, Android, and a static web export) running entirely on mock data — no backend. A fixed demo clock puts the fan mid-tournament: Wed 23 June 2027, 12:55 EAT, standing in Kasarani, Nairobi, holding an active Pass.

ThingWhat it is
The PassThe credential. Identity, entitlements, validity. Rendered from local state only — never a network call.
The recordAn append-only list of discounts claimed. Held on the device. Drives every "you've saved" figure.
The ticketA seat, distinct from the credential. Category, gate, section, seat.
Partners2,189 businesses giving Pass holders a discount, in six categories.
Fixtures11 seeded matches across three countries and six venues.

Two hues only — deep and accent — sampled from the proposal artwork and matching the Uratibu brand guideline exactly (Forest, Command Cerulean, Tint, Paper, Ink). There is no per-category or per-status colour coding. Where something has tiers, tier is shown as depth of the one accent (100% / 55% / 25%); a disabled or sold-out state leaves the hue entirely for panel.

Type. Outfit carries voice; JetBrains Mono carries anything procedural — codes, amounts, record lines, timestamps, pass numbers — uppercased for eyebrows. The split is the point: a glance tells narrative text from data you might read aloud, type in, or match against a physical document. Families are addressed by semantic key (font-sans, font-medium, font-display, font-display-heavy, font-mono, font-mono-medium), never by weight utility.

Shape and motif. Cards use a 10px radius. All media is cropped to Mount Kenya's split summit — Batian, the Gate of the Mists, Nelion — from one geometry table, which also produces the Explore tab icon. Media only: cards, chips and panels stay rectangular.

Navigation model

5 tabs, each owning its own stack — a pushed screen stays under the tab bar.

Home     ── Partner ── Confirm
Explore  ── Partner ── Confirm ── TicketOffice
Live
Services ── Category ── Partner ── Confirm ── Scan ── Parking ── Safety ── GettingThere
Pass     ── Wallet

Above all five: Issuance, shown only when no Pass exists.

Screens reachable from more than one tab are registered in each of those stacks, so every tab keeps its own history. Back is a circular chevron at the top-left, which hides itself at a stack root. Verified sound: the tab bar is present on all 15 screens and back works on every pushed screen.

Problems to solve — evidence, not opinion

1. Seven of eleven fixtures cannot be reached. Explore is the app's only fixture list, and it is capped to a seven-day window and five rows. Home shows one fixture; Live shows what is live or next. There is no full schedule anywhere. A fan cannot see who plays next week, and a match dated outside the window is invisible app-wide — this was discovered when a fixture had to be re-dated to make its own ticket office reachable at all.
2. Assistive-tech roles — fixed 19 Aug 2026, no design needed. Eleven of the app's fifteen pressables had no accessibilityRole, so every interactive row, tile and pill was announced as plain content: a screen with twelve controls reported one. Roles and selected/disabled states are now in place (measured 1 → 12 on Getting there). What is still a design question is the visible side: focus rings and pressed states are currently opacity changes only.
3. Getting there stacks three rows of chrome before content. Drive/Fly, then Arriving/Leaving, then up to five route pills. Three decisions before the fan sees a single fact, and two of the three are modal.
4. Services is a grab-bag. Six service tiles, a getting-there row, a partner count, and six category tiles on one screen — transport, food, lodging, safety, retail and cross-border travel under one word.
5. Desktop is a phone in a column. The rail works, but nothing reflows: no screen uses more than one column, so a 1440px viewport shows the same single-file list a 390px phone does.
6. Affordances are inconsistent. One fixture row opens a ticket office; the other three look identical and do nothing. The office is reachable from exactly one place in the app.

What to design

Mobile

  • An information architecture that exposes the whole schedule, and a route from any fixture to what a fan can do about it — travel, tickets, the ground.
  • A better answer than three stacked toggles for "how do I get to a match", covering road and air, in and out of Nairobi.
  • A split or restructure of Services that separates matchday logistics from the 2,189-partner discount network.
  • Visible focus rings and pressed states for every control — the semantics are now correct, the visual feedback is still just an opacity change.

Desktop

  • A layout that earns its width: multi-column where content is list-like, and a persistent secondary pane where a fan is comparing things (routes, blocks, offers).
  • A place for the rail to carry more than five words.

The agreed target design

Brainstormed 19 August 2026. The three decisions below are settled; the visual design of them is what this brief is asking for.

1. The match is the spine

Almost everything a fan does hangs off a specific fixture — where it is, how to get there, tickets, parking, the ground. Today the fixture is a row buried under a search box and the things attached to it are scattered across three tabs. A Matches tab holds the full schedule; tapping a fixture opens one screen that composes everything about it:

MATCH DETAIL
  TeamRow (flags + names) · kickoff · venue AND city
  State ......... scheduled -> live (score, stats) -> full-time     [absorbs the Live tab]
  Getting there . road + air legs to THIS city, with cost estimates
  Tickets ....... hall map + block picker, where a map exists
  At the ground . parking zones + walk times, safety, shuttles
  Your ticket ... if the Pass carries one for this match

2. Four tabs

TabHolds
HomeToday: a live match if one is live, the next fixture, savings, offers near you, and the arriving-in-Kenya travel guide.
MatchesThe full schedule, grouped by day. Row chips: LIVE / TICKETS / FLY / IN N DAYS. Leads to match detail.
OffersThe 2,189-partner network: search, six categories, nearby, and the redemption flow.
PassThe credential, what it unlocks, the match ticket, and the record.

Live stops being a tab — it is dead most of the tournament — and becomes a state of a match. Services splits: matchday logistics attach to the match they belong to, and the discount network becomes Offers, which is what fans actually go looking for.

3. Desktop is two-pane master/detail

+------+------------------+---------------------------+
| rail | MATCHES          | Kenya v Mali              |
| Home | > Sat 26 Jun     | Kasarani, Nairobi         |
| Match|   Mon 28 Jun     | Gates open 14:00          |
| Offer|   Tue 29 Jun     | Getting there / Tickets   |
| Pass |   Wed 30 Jun     | At the ground             |
+------+------------------+---------------------------+

Almost every screen in this app is a list that leads to a detail, so the list and the detail should be visible together above 1024px: schedule beside a match, offers beside a partner, the Pass beside its record. Home stays a single wide surface, but multi-column.

Two tensions this creates, to design around

Live needs a one-tap path. Folding it into a match is right, but during a match a fan must not have to go Matches → find the fixture → scroll. Home has to surface a live match loudly and lead straight to it.
Inbound travel is not match-specific. "Arriving in Kenya from Uganda" belongs to a fan, not a fixture. Per-match travel lives in the detail; the arriving guide needs its own home off Home or it is orphaned.

Non-negotiables

  • No payment, ever. Pamoja never holds funds, never sees a card number, needs no banking licence. Money flows are hand-offs: the fan pays the merchant, the seller, or the airline directly.
  • Two hues. No third brand colour, no per-category or per-status colour coding.
  • Outfit and JetBrains Mono, addressed by semantic key.
  • No invented data presented as fact. Prices, fares, schedules and entry requirements carry an "as of" date and a confirm-before-you-travel caveat, or are labelled prototype figures. Nothing may imply a precision it does not have.
  • The record is local. No dashboard anywhere assembles a view of a fan.
